Google Chromecast Voice Remote

Model: G9N9N · 2 AAA batteries
Voice and buttons supported
  • Known hardware versions & integration paths (all report VID/PID 18D1:9450 · check HID version, firmware, and Button Integration in Connection settings after pairing): 1) 0x0110 / FW 26.2 (PVT/MP): Standard System HID, plug-and-play voice + all 14 buttons; 2) A0 / FW 26.7 and 0x0110 / FW 26.0 (CR PVT/MP, per #256 ↗): Full Support · Log Recovery (single-key reports do not reach native HID; installing the bundled .mobileconfig profile and enabling recovery unlocks all 14 buttons and voice); 3) 0x011B (zhuhai_jieli compatible batch, per button report #7 ↗ and voice cutoff #280 ↗): Buttons via Log Recovery · ~60s Voice Cap (macOS drops short Report 2 packets; installing the bundled .mobileconfig profile and enabling recovery enables all 14 buttons; ATVV voice works, but stops silently around ~60.6s with MIC_EXTEND ignored).
    In principle, log recovery can bypass macOS restrictions to support any G9N9N hardware variant, though previously unseen special revisions still require dedicated packet-format adaptation.
  • Button mapping: Supports single, double, and long-press actions plus common phrases across all 14 ordinary buttons (directions, OK, Back, Home, Mute, volume, YouTube, Netflix, Input, and Power), as well as per-Application overrides with built-in Safari and Chrome browser presets.
  • Voice, duration & LED: Defaults to Smart Toggle—tap and release to speak hands-free (press again or tap another button to stop, ~0.2s mic reopen pause), or hold over 0.7s to stop on release; can also switch to pure Hold to Talk in Connection settings. Host auto-stop timeout is configurable in General settings (3 / 5 / 10 / 15 / 30 min, 5 min default), while >60s keepalive depends on batch firmware: 0x0110 / FW 26.2 is verified to stream continuously past 60s (68.9s via MIC_EXTEND); 0x011B (zhuhai_jieli, per #280 ↗) ignores MIC_EXTEND and stops silently around 60.6s (no AUDIO_STOP), remaining capped at ~60s in the current version; 26.7 / 26.0 await long-duration testing. The bottom white LED glows steadily while streaming voice; battery percentage appears when a reading is available.
  • Setup & defaults: Press OK, then test three different ordinary buttons. Global defaults include Mute (Return on single press, Send / Command+Return on long press), YouTube (previous track), Netflix (next track), and Input (play/pause); built-in Safari and Chrome profiles add Up/Down scroll, Back/Forward (Command+[ / ]), Reload (Command+R), and Close Tab (Command+W). All mappings can be customized.

Xiaomi Bluetooth Remote 2 / 2 Pro

Model: RC001 / RC003 · Direct Bluetooth
Supported
  • 2 vs. 2 Pro: Remote 2 (RC001) and 2 Pro (RC003) share the same voice, button, and Bluetooth functionality. The main difference is power—Remote 2 uses replaceable batteries, while 2 Pro has a built-in 300mAh battery charged via bottom USB-C.
  • Build: Slim brushed-metal body (2 Pro 138 × 35 × 8.9 mm, ~61 g), circular D-pad, and crisp tactile keys.
  • Voice trigger: Hold the voice button to speak and release to finish (~60s single-hold cap). Crisp walkie-talkie feel with no need to remember a second press to stop.
  • Button mapping: Remappable single, double, and long presses. Defaults: Home switches apps, TV plays/pauses media, Menu opens context menu, Power sends Backspace, Back sends Escape.
  • Buying note: 2 Pro is listed around ¥99 CNY (~$14 USD; ~¥60+ CNY on sales) in mainland China. If buying Remote 2, verify the model label reads RC001 rather than an IR-only variant.

T1 Air-Mouse & Keyboard Remote

Model: T1-Remote · Direct Bluetooth (not 2.4G dongle)
Supported
  • 3-in-1 hardware: 164 × 50 × 11 mm, ~62 g (400mAh, right-side USB-C). Front features 7 remappable buttons (Back, Home, Menu, Vol+, Vol−, Mute, Power); rear features a ~45-key QWERTY mini keyboard plus a 6-axis gyro air mouse passing through natively to macOS.
  • Hands-free toggle: On the tested Bluetooth sample, press once to start talking, then press again to stop (tested nearly 2 minutes continuously; keepalive every 10s, with auto-stop timeout configurable in General as 3 / 5 / 10 / 15 / 30 min, 5 min default; clone batches require individual testing for >60s support). Works seamlessly with both Hold-mode and Toggle-mode input tools.
  • Buying note: Commonly ~¥50 CNY (~$7 USD) on Taobao. Hold Home + Back to pair over direct Bluetooth (2.4 GHz USB receivers do not carry ATVV audio). Lookalike OEM batches vary—buy from a seller offering returns.

CURRENTLY USABLE DEVICES · VISUAL COMPARISON

Three-remote comparison

Xiaomi Bluetooth Remote 2 Pro, Google Chromecast G9N9N, and T1 Air-Mouse & Keyboard Voice Remote all provide voice input, with different physical voice-button behavior and ordinary-button support.

CHOOSE BY HOW IT FEELS

Buying guide

For everyday voice input and shortcuts, prefer a G9N9N remote. Choose T1 for its keyboard and air mouse, or Xiaomi for its metal build. These handling comparisons come from hands-on use.

FIRST CHOICE

G9N9N: Smart Toggle, comfortable, great value

Supports both Smart Toggle (tap hands-free) and Hold to Talk; verified units such as 0x0110 / FW 26.2 exceed 60s via keepalive (auto-stop timeout configurable in General, 5 min default; compatible batches like 0x011B still have a ~60s firmware cutoff), and the bottom white LED shows active streaming. Shorter than Xiaomi with a rounded grip, very light, and around ¥15 (~$2), with Voice positioned right below the directional ring for effortless thumb reach.

For profile-free plug-and-play setup and verified >60s keepalive, prefer the tested 0x0110 / FW 26.2 batch. The remote uses two replaceable AAA batteries.

MORE FEATURES

T1: keyboard, air mouse, and voice

An affordable choice with a rear mini keyboard and air mouse for large-screen control, cursor movement, and occasional typing. Press Voice once to start and again to stop, without holding it throughout.

It is longer and wider than the other two. Choose it for multiple input methods; its built-in battery charges over USB-C.

METAL BUILD

Xiaomi 2 Pro: finish and rechargeability

The metal body and finish are its strengths, alongside convenient USB-C charging. Its longer body places Voice at the upper-right end, requiring more thumb reach, with less advantage in price or handling than G9N9N.

Best for people who prefer a metal feel and a built-in rechargeable battery. For affordability and easy thumb reach, start with G9N9N.

G9N9N and Xiaomi: voice-button reach, grip, and thickness

Length and voice-button reach: G9N9N is on the left, Xiaomi 2 Pro on the right. G9N9N places Voice below the direction ring, within easy thumb reach. Xiaomi's longer body puts Voice at its upper-right end, making it harder to reach while holding the remote.

Thickness and grip: G9N9N is thicker, with a rounded back and a fuller grip. Xiaomi is thinner with a metal finish. G9N9N takes AAA batteries; Xiaomi 2 Pro charges over USB-C.

Xiaomi and T1 in photos

Front view: Xiaomi 2 Pro (left) has a slim metal finish; T1 (right) is wider and moves volume + USB-C to its right edge.

Port placement: Xiaomi places USB-C on the bottom end, whereas T1 places USB-C and volume keys along the right edge.

Footprint: Xiaomi 2 Pro measures 138 × 35 × 8.9 mm; T1 measures 164 × 50 × 11 mm. Xiaomi is noticeably shorter and narrower.

Thickness: Xiaomi 2 Pro is 8.9 mm thick, compared with 11 mm for T1. Their port placement also differs.

T1 Dual-Sided Layout & Controls

The front face provides 7 remappable control keys (Back, Home, Menu, Vol+, Vol−, Mute, Power) for triggering custom shortcuts while keeping D-pad and OK native. Flip the remote over to type on a ~45-key QWERTY mini keyboard or steer the Mac pointer with its built-in 6-axis gyro air mouse.
